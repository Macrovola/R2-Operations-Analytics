2.1  ODC position & procurement controls

The Supply Order Authorization (SOA) cycle is the controlled path by which a Performance Center converts a validated field need into a Government-authorized, reimbursed purchase. I own the finance review, data normalization, and the trackers that turn each authorized order into estimated-versus-incurred dollars and roll it up — by location and month — into the Government-facing TOSR and A&L briefs.

The result is continuous burn-rate visibility: leadership always sees committed spend and remaining runway against the ceiling in one place, and every purchase carries written justification, dual authorization, and a defensible audit trail.

Executive Summary

EXSUM // R2 PMO Operations AnalyticsHandling: UNCLASSIFIED // Portfolio
VERDICT: Fiscal stewardship is demonstrated and disciplined. Spend on the CST26 event landed 23.7% under authorization at $27.78 per cadet, and the $2.72M ODC ceiling is governed by an auditable eight-step cycle holding 83.2% runway. The open risk is effectiveness: utilization and outcome metrics are not yet captured, so return on the discretionary tail is asserted, not yet proven.

5.1 Facts

Four chained trackers (MTT, local travel, site visits, SOA) reconcile projected vs. incurred into one YTD roll-up. CST26: 191 training-days across 8 deployments; $55,554.66 incurred (76.3% of the $72,821.29 authorization); ~$27.78/cadet; 67.6% fixed / 32.4% discretionary. FY26 ODC ceiling $2.72M, $468,619 committed, 83.2% remaining.

5.2 Interpretation

Cost is governed by travel logistics, not daily rates, and came in well under authorization — fiscally efficient. Procurement is defensible by design, and the reporting layer was modernized: A&L briefs automated with J. Choun and the SOA SOP standardized across all 32 sites.

5.3 Recommendation

Accept the spend as justified. Next cycle, pair the fiscal roll-up with utilization/outcome metrics and bank savings from the 32.4% tail via rental pooling, fly-vs-drive discipline, and single-packet control.
